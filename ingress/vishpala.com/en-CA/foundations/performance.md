Speed is not a trick applied at the end. It comes from sending less: fewer bytes, fewer requests, and no framework that has to boot before the page appears.

## Static by default

Pages are built ahead of time into plain HTML. When a visitor arrives, the server hands over a finished document — there is no application to start, no data to fetch before anything is readable. The first thing the browser receives is the content itself.

## Very little JavaScript

The site works without JavaScript for its core reading experience. What little script exists loads on demand rather than up front, so the page is usable long before any enhancement arrives. Nothing blocks the text from rendering.

## Self-hosted fonts

Typography is served from the site's own domain, subset to the characters actually used. There is no round-trip to a font provider, no flash of invisible text while a third party responds, and no dependency on a network you do not control.

## Why it matters

A fast, light page is not only pleasant on a fast connection — it is the difference between usable and unusable on a slow one, on an older phone, or on a metered plan where every megabyte has a cost. Performance here is a form of inclusion, and it feeds directly into the *Sustainability* pillar of the Universal Cake Evaluation Metrics.
